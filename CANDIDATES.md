# Candidates — One Word From Your Ex (u-brave-eagle-12-one-word-from-your-ex-cfd97ba2)

Judge (z-ai/glm-5.3-flash, pairwise, no ties) weighs, in order:
1. Exactly one word an ex would use to describe you — a real word a person
   would say about a partner they once had.
2. Makes me laugh out loud, through surprise, a sharp turn, or a word that
   lands harder than expected.
3. Stings with recognition — painfully true, and the entrant plainly knows it.
4. Lands instantly, needs no explanation, cannot be swapped for a near
   synonym without losing its edge.
5. Feels relatable — the kind of word people repeat because they have lived
   through it too.
A word that stings because it is true beats a word that only insults.
Meanness for its own sake does not count.

## Local pairwise tournament (judge criteria, in order)
- Phase beats Roommate: shorter, more universal (no cohabitation needed),
  same laugh, sharper dismissal; Roommate is the crowd's pick.
- Phase beats Practice: Practice needs the "practice boyfriend" phrase to
  complete the joke; Phase is complete alone.
- Phase beats Placeholder: Placeholder stings harder but laughs less;
  the judge weighs laugh and sting together.
- Phase beats Almost: Almost is sad-funny; Phase is laugh-first, and the
  sting is still there ("just a phase" = forgettable).
- Phase beats Rebound: Rebound swaps for "fling" too easily (criterion 4);
  no synonym replaces "phase".
- Phase beats Lesson: Lesson's sting is softened by growth; Phase keeps
  the sting (you were temporary).
- Phase beats Pitstop / Downgrade / Homework: each needs a beat of
  explanation or leans on meanness; Phase lands in one beat.

## Choice: Phase
- Criterion 1: "He was just a phase" is exactly how an ex describes a
  person they once had — a real word, real voice.
- Criterion 2: the laugh is the brutal compression — years of a person
  reduced to a passing stage.
- Criterion 3: the sting is recognition — being forgettable, temporary,
  outgrown; the entrant plainly knows it.
- Criterion 4: lands instantly; "stage", "period", "moment" do not carry
  the same dismissal.
- Criterion 5: everyone has been a phase, or had one — the most repeated
  ex-verdict there is.
- 5 bytes: wins the exact-tie rule (smaller text places first), and the
  earliest receipt in an empty field is an extra edge.
- Distinctive enough not to be a sealed anchor/rung (a plain generic word);
  "Phase" as an ex's verdict is a specific joke, not a house calibration word.
- Runner-ups: Roommate, Practice, Placeholder.
